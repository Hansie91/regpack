# 05 - Evidence, idempotency, duplicate prevention and recovery

Research brief for the regulatory/financial reporting platform design spec. Research date: 2026-10-08. "Fetched" means the page was read in this session on that date; the document's own date is given where known. Confidence tags: H = primary source read, M = primary read via a mirror/secondary or partially verified, L = secondary only or from memory (flagged).

## 1. Summary

1. **Evidence is a pipeline property, not a log table.** No regulation prescribes an "evidence format". The requirement is assembled from: record-retention rules (MiFIR Art 25, MiFID II Art 16, EMIR Art 9(2), SFTR Art 4(4), CFTC 45.2), record-quality rules (MiFID Org Reg Art 72(1): reconstitute each key stage, prior contents of corrections ascertainable, not possible to alter), reconciliation/notification duties (RTS 22 Art 15, ESMA EMIR Guidelines, CFTC 45.14) and supervisory findings (FCA Market Watch 74/81: missing audit trail of errors and fixes, weak data lineage).
2. **Recommended shape: two tiers.** (a) An *operational ledger* in a transactional DB (state machine, queues, bi-temporal decisions) and (b) an *evidence tier* of byte-exact artifacts, responses and signed, periodically anchored hash-chain/Merkle checkpoints on WORM object storage (S3 Object Lock compliance mode or Azure locked immutability policy). The DB is rebuildable from the evidence tier; the evidence tier is never rebuilt.
3. **Idempotency = business key + monotonic version + content hash + single-in-flight guard.** The regimes already give natural keys (EMIR: UTI per reporting counterparty; MiFIR: executing-entity ID + TRN). Endpoints reject some duplicates but acknowledgements are asynchronous, so delivery is at-least-once with a durable write-ahead "attempt started" record and an explicit IN_DOUBT state.
4. **Recovery hinges on IN_DOUBT resolution against the endpoint's own feedback** (TR end-of-day reports, ARM/NCA feedback files, MDP extracts, SDR messages) - not on trusting the restored DB. Restore -> verify chain against external anchor -> freeze sending -> reconcile -> release, all recorded as ledger events.
5. **Store choice:** PostgreSQL (>=16, target 18) is the safest default for the operational ledger; SQLite + Litestream is a credible low-ops profile only where async replication (RPO > 0, single node) is acceptable. immudb (BSL 1.1 with an embedding restriction), KurrentDB (source-available licence), FoundationDB (ops-heavy) and Kafka (not an event store) fit poorly with "minimal lifecycle management". AWS retired QLDB on 31 Jul 2025 - a warning against proprietary ledger databases.
6. **Fast-moving ground (2025-2026):** DORA applying since 17 Jan 2025; ESMA left RTS 22/24 unchanged (Jun 2025) and proposed "report once" (2 Jul 2026); FCA PS26/15 (3 Aug 2026) cuts UK fields 65->52 and back-reporting 5->3 years from 3 Apr 2028; EMIR Refit reconciliation Phase II (27 Apr 2026); EDPB blockchain guidelines v2 (7 Jul 2026); pgBackRest archived then revived (Apr-May 2026). See section 3.

## 4. Detailed findings

### 4.1 What regulators expect as "evidence of what was reported"
- **Reproducible state, not just logs.** Org Reg Art 72(1) (K3) and CFTC 45.2/45.14 (K6) require that each key processing stage can be reconstituted, prior contents of corrections are visible, and records cannot be altered. SEC 17a-4(f) (K7) is the most explicit technical statement (WORM *or* full audit trail with re-creation of the original).
- **Reconciliation against the endpoint is a duty, and the ledger must prove it ran.** RTS 22 Art 15 (reconcile front office vs NCA samples), ESMA EMIR Guidelines para 382 (act on rejections *and* reconciliation feedback; ITS requires arrangements to take it into account), CFTC 45.14(b) (verification logged). FCA Market Watch 81 criticises partial-field/irregular reconciliations and missing data lineage.
- **Error/omission notifications carry scope data:** affected reports, cause, remediation timeline (ESMA para 384-385; RTS 22 Art 15(2); CFTC 12-hour notice). The ledger should be able to produce "which reports, when, which version, what status" instantly.
- **ARM-delegated corrections** must be recorded by the firm (RTS 22 Art 15(6)): the ledger needs to ingest ARM-side cancel/correct details, not just own submissions.
- **Back-reporting horizons** (5 years today; 3 under UK PS26/15 from 2028, proposed in EU) set how far back "as known at T" reproduction must work operationally, independent of retention.

### 4.2 Event log vs transactional outbox
Not either/or. The submission lifecycle is naturally an **append-only event stream per delivery** (events: queued, attempt_started, response_received, ...), while upstream business data (reference data, config) is ordinary CRUD with bi-temporal versioning. Event sourcing for the entire domain is costly to adopt/migrate and rarely justified (K22). The **outbox** guarantees that "decision made" and "send intent recorded" commit atomically; the relay is at-least-once, so everything downstream (our sender, the endpoint, our response handler) must be idempotent. A Kafka log can be an *input/transport* but not the ledger (no per-key optimistic concurrency).

### 4.3 Tamper evidence, WORM, timestamps
- **Hash chain per stream** (`entry_hash = SHA-256(prev_hash || canonical entry)`) is cheap and detects edits/deletes only if the *head is externally anchored*. **Merkle tree** (RFC 9162 hashing) adds O(log n) inclusion/consistency proofs, so one report's evidence can be proven to an auditor without shipping the whole ledger. Both are tamper-**evident**, not tamper-proof (K20).
- **Anchoring tiers:** (1) signed checkpoint (tree size, root, UTC time) written as a new object to a WORM bucket; (2) optional RFC 3161 token from a TSA for independent time evidence; (3) optional external witness (C2SP style). Whether an RFC 3161 token or eIDAS "qualified" time stamp/ledger carries a legal presumption was **not verified** (see section 6).
- **WORM options:** S3 Object Lock (compliance mode; variable retention suits "5 years after termination" clocks whose start is unknown at write time) and Azure locked time-based retention (version-level for per-object periods; extension allowed, shortening not; no PITR on same account). Both are Cohasset-assessed against SEC 17a-4(f)/CFTC/FINRA (K8, K9). Compliance-mode mistakes are unrecoverable (wrong retention can only be extended, not removed) - use governance mode in non-production and a staged rollout.
- **Alternatives to a WORM bucket** are possible under 17a-4(f)(2)(i)(A) (complete audit trail) but that is a US broker-dealer rule; for EU/UK, Org Reg Art 72 is outcome-based, so WORM is sufficient but not strictly mandated (inference).

### 4.4 Bi-temporal reproduction ("as known at T")
Track **valid time** (business effective/event date, trade date) separately from **system time** (when the ledger recorded it) and never update rows - corrections append a row with `supersedes_id` (Fowler, K23). To reproduce a past report you need, pinned per decision: input snapshot hashes, **rule-bundle version** (DRR/CDM release + local overrides; ISDA publishes no version scheme, K24, so we mint our own content-hash ID), **reference-data snapshot ID** (LEI/ISIN/CFI/MIC/FIRDS state as of the decision), XSD + validation-rule version (ESMA EMIR rules dated 6 Sep 2023, XSDs 27 Nov 2023 - K10), mapping version and engine build. Reproduction test = re-run the pinned pipeline on the stored inputs and compare artifact SHA-256 to the recorded one; a mismatch is a determinism defect. PostgreSQL 18 `WITHOUT OVERLAPS`/`PERIOD` enforces non-overlapping validity ranges for reference-data tables (K16); SQLite needs app-level enforcement.

### 4.5 Duplicate prevention
| Regime | Business key | Verified duplicate/ordering behaviour |
|--------|--------------|----------------------------------------|
| EMIR Refit | UTI + reporting counterparty (dual-sided) | Per-counterparty action-type state machine; Art 9(1e) no duplication; one NEWT/POSC per UTI (DTCC presenter via Qomply, L); TR change/M&A/delegation change are duplicate-risk events (K11) |
| MiFIR | executing entity ID + TRN | Duplicate record = content error; NEWT/CANC alternation; TRN reused only for replacement (K12, K13) |
| CFTC | USI/UTI + reporting counterparty | SDR must send acceptance/error message "as soon as technologically practicable"; obligation stays unmet until validation passes (17 CFR 49.10, secondary summary, M) |
| SFTR | UTI | Not researched in detail |

Pattern (design inference): `report_version(business_key, version_seq)` monotonic; `artifact_sha256` of the exact bytes; a content hash over the *logical* report to suppress no-op re-sends; unique constraints on both; **partial unique index permitting one in-flight delivery per (endpoint, business_key)**. Where the endpoint lacks idempotency support, "effectively once" = at-least-once send + write-ahead intent + endpoint-side natural dedupe (rejects NEWT duplicates) + **query-before-resend** against the endpoint's feedback/state report. Because EMIR reports are full-state and the TR orders by event date/reporting timestamp (K11), an in-doubt v1 can be **superseded** by v2 instead of resent - but v1's fate must still be resolved and recorded for evidence.

### 4.6 Submission ledger: what to store per attempt
Attempt id; delivery id; attempt_no; **payload SHA-256 + byte size + object ref**; format/schema/XSD version; endpoint identity (host/path/channel, TLS peer fingerprint, credentials *reference* not secret); protocol correlation IDs (file name, BAH `BizMsgIdr`/`Related`, TR submission id); timestamps: queued, intent_logged, first_byte_sent, last_byte_sent, response_received (UTC, plus monotonic delta and clock-sync status); transport result class (`FAILED_BEFORE_SEND` | `FAILED_AFTER_SEND_UNKNOWN` | `TRANSPORT_OK`); raw response bytes (hash + object ref); parsed status/codes; operator or service identity; software build. "Failed before any byte left" is safely retryable; anything after the first byte is IN_DOUBT until evidence says otherwise.

### 4.7 Submission state model (per delivery of one report version)
`PREPARED` (artifact built, pre-validated, hash fixed) -> `QUEUED` (outbox, ordered per business key) -> `SENDING` (intent row durable **before** network I/O) -> `AWAITING_ACK` (transport OK; ack deadline timer) -> terminal `ACCEPTED` / `ACCEPTED_WITH_WARNING`; `REJECTED_FILE` / `REJECTED_RECORD` (error codes stored) -> `REPAIR_QUEUE` (data/rules fix -> new `report_version`, never edit the old one); `PENDING_AT_ENDPOINT` (MiFIR PDNG/RCVD, resolve <= 7 days); `IN_DOUBT` (timeout, crash, restore, overdue ack); `SUPERSEDED`; `WITHDRAWN` (with reason). Retryable transport failures go to `RESEND_QUEUE` with backoff. `ack_deadline` per endpoint profile: EMIR 60 min + EOD file by 06:00 UTC next working day (K10); MiFIR R+1, PDNG <= 7 days (K12). Absence of feedback is **neither success nor failure** (K12). All transitions are events with cause, evidence ref and actor; current state is a projection.

### 4.8 Recovery algorithm (concrete)
1. **Declare restore epoch.** Record `restore_epoch+1` (backup id, PITR target time/LSN, operator, reason). Restore into an isolated, segregated environment first (DORA Art 12(3)); verify backup integrity; only then promote.
2. **Verify ledger integrity:** recompute hash chain/Merkle from the last good checkpoint; compare head with the newest externally anchored checkpoint on WORM. If restored head < anchored head, the gap `(restored_seq, anchored_seq]` is *known data loss*: replay it from the WORM journal (journal objects hold every event of the gap). If no journal covers it, widen the unknown window (step 4).
3. **Freeze outbound** for affected endpoints (circuit breaker); keep inbound feedback ingestion running.
4. **Build the in-doubt set:** all deliveries in `SENDING`/`AWAITING_ACK`/`IN_DOUBT`/overdue at restore point, plus any delivery whose attempt-start is not in the restored DB but exists as endpoint-side evidence within the loss window (time window = restore point minus safety margin to now).
5. **Fetch authoritative endpoint state** per regime: EMIR/SFTR TR end-of-day reports (rejections auth.092, reconciliation, trade-state report, activity report - names per TR docs, Regis-TR lists auth.091/092/107/030) and portal search by UTI; MiFIR ARM/NCA feedback files and (UK) MDP extract (1 request/day - plan for this); CFTC SDR acceptance/error messages and verification data.
6. **Resolve each in-doubt delivery** by matching `(business_key, version_seq/action, artifact hash or reporting timestamp)`:
   - Evidence of acceptance -> `ACCEPTED` (attach evidence object) [may carry `resolved_by=restore_reconciliation`].
   - Evidence of rejection -> `REJECTED_*` -> repair queue.
   - Evidence of pending -> `PENDING_AT_ENDPOINT`.
   - Newer version already queued/accepted -> `SUPERSEDED` (still record what v1's fate was).
   - Not found **and** beyond the endpoint's documented feedback horizon -> `RESEND_QUEUE` (re-send same artifact bytes, preserving order per key).
   - Not found but inside horizon -> remain `IN_DOUBT`, re-check on schedule; never resend yet.
   - A "duplicate" rejection on a resend is evidence the original was accepted -> resolve to ACCEPTED only after confirming with the TR/NCA state report (design inference).
7. **Reconcile totals:** ledger counts per endpoint/day vs endpoint activity/trade-state reports; run the invariant suite (no key with two in-flight deliveries; every ACCEPTED has a response artifact whose hash exists in WORM; no sequence gaps; per-key version continuity).
8. **Release sending** per endpoint once its in-doubt set is empty or only "inside horizon" items remain that are blocked from resend; write a signed **restore report** (scope, RPO achieved, items resolved by evidence, items resent) to WORM.
9. **Notify as required:** reporting obstacle to NCAs (ESMA EMIR Guidelines para 384b), RTS 22 Art 15(2) prompt notification, CFTC 45.14 12-hour notice, DORA incident process - the ledger supplies affected-report lists.
10. **Practice it:** scheduled restore drills with fault-injected crash points (after intent, after first byte, after response, mid-restore) satisfy DORA Art 12(2) testing and are ideal TDD fixtures.

**RPO/RTO (proposal, not a legal number - DORA leaves per-function targets to the firm):** RPO 0 for intent/attempt/response events (synchronous replica commit or journal-first write to WORM; per-file events are low-volume even when records are millions), <= 1 min for bulk per-record projection data; RTO measured in hours, well inside the T+1 deadlines (EMIR: working day following the event; MiFIR Art 26(1): close of following working day).

Termination-anchored clocks mean `retain_until` is **not known at write time**: store regime, anchor event and computed date as ledger fields; use S3 variable retention/legal hold or Azure legal hold/extension. **GDPR:** MiFIR/EMIR reports contain natural-person identifiers; Art 17(3)(b) covers erasure refusals *during* the legal retention period only; after expiry delete. Keep personal data out of the chain: chain/Merkle leaves hold keyed hashes (HMAC) of ciphertext + non-personal metadata; payload artifacts are envelope-encrypted so expiry/crypto-shredding never breaks verification. EDPB v2 (K21) is blockchain-specific, so this is an analogy - get DPO sign-off.

### 4.10 DORA and BCBS 239 touchpoints
DORA Art 12 maps to: documented backup policy and frequency; periodic restore tests; restore on segregated systems; per-function RTO/RPO; post-recovery integrity checks and reconciliations (our step 7); redundancy for non-micro entities (a single SQLite node needs a documented rationale). Our platform is likely an ICT third-party service to customers, who will demand exit/portability (open formats, documented restore). **BCBS 239** (9 Jan 2013, BIS) is a bank risk-data aggregation standard; its accuracy/integrity, adaptability and documented-data-flow themes are a useful lineage checklist, but principle/paragraph text was **not** re-verified (BIS PDF unreadable by tooling; ECB 2024 guide not retrieved) - do not cite paragraph numbers from this brief.

## 5. Design implications for our platform
1. Make **"attempt intent durable before I/O"** and **"IN_DOUBT"** first-class; unit-test every crash point (TDD fits: state machine and recovery are pure functions over events).
2. Treat the **TR/NCA feedback as data to ingest and reconcile**, with per-endpoint profiles (ack deadlines, status vocab, error-code tables, duplicate semantics) loaded as configuration (supports R8-style configurability). Vocabulary differs by regime/TR (ESMA ISO 20022 XSDs vs MiFIR NCA statuses vs SDR messages); never hard-code one.
3. **Per-key ordering and supersession** in the sender; preserve NEWT->CANC->NEWT alternation (MiFIR) and the EMIR action-type state machine; coalesce redundant in-flight versions.
4. **Pin versions** (rule bundle, reference data, XSD/validation rules, mapping, build) on every decision; mint our own content-hash IDs because DRR/CDM publishes none visibly.
5. **Evidence export**: per report or date range, a signed bundle (artifact, transport proof, feedback, decision + lineage, corrections chain, reconciliation results, inclusion proof + anchor).
6. **Retention engine** computing `retain_until` per regime/anchor event; supports legal hold; GDPR-safe layout (personal data only in encrypted artifacts).
7. **Operational reports for supervisors:** affected-report lists for error/omission and obstacle notifications.
8. Keep storage behind a narrow repository interface but ship **one** primary store; avoid proprietary ledger DBs and BSL/source-available embedded licences.
9. Plan for **regulatory churn**: EU report-once (not before H2 2031 if adopted), UK 2028 regime, EMIR reconciliation phases - the ledger schema is regime-agnostic (business key + action vocabulary per profile).

### Corrections that would change a design decision

1. Retention basis: MiFID II Art 16(6) has no retention period; the 5-year / up-to-7-year sentence is in Art 16(7) and covers communications recordings. The retention engine should key transaction/decision/evidence records to MiFIR Art 25 (5 y), EMIR Art 9(2) / SFTR Art 4(4) / CFTC 45.2 (termination-anchored) and national law, and offer the 7-year NCA-request extension as a legal-hold style override, not as a general Art 16(6) clock.
2. immudb licence: the Additional Use Grant restriction is conditional ("in order to compete with Codenotary's paid version(s)"), not a blanket ban on hosted/embedded use. The "avoid" recommendation can stay (BSL, per-version Change Date, legal review) but the brief should not say it "bars" embedding.
3. Back-reporting horizon: the 5-to-3-year cut is a short-medium term Level 2/3 recommendation in the EU (not an H2 2031 item) and is firm in the UK from 3 Apr 2028. Make the back-reporting horizon an effective-dated per-regime setting separate from record retention (ESMA itself ties the 5-year default to record-keeping).
4. EMIR reconciliation: field sets are effective-dated (87 fields from 29 Apr 2024; 148 after the second phase, about 29 Apr 2026 by the ESMA workbook formula, 27 Apr per a secondary source). Model the reconcilable-field set per TR and date rather than a constant.
5. EMIR feedback deadlines: the end-of-day rejection file is due 6:00 UTC for entities and 12:00 UTC for authorities, using the TARGET2 calendar for working days. Endpoint profiles should carry a calendar, not assume local business days.
6. Supervisory evidence claim: Market Watch 81 supports reconciliation, data-lineage and record-keeping findings but not "missing audit trail of errors and fixes"; do not cite it for that. The audit-trail design remains justified by Org Reg Art 72(1)(a)-(c) (UK as-adopted text) and, for US scope, 17a-4(f).
7. S3 variable retention: do not assume the Cohasset 17a-4 assessment covers event-hold/variable retention. For termination-anchored clocks keep a fallback to fixed retain-until-dates that are only ever extended, and confirm Cohasset scope with the vendor before claiming 17a-4(f) posture.
8. UK legal basis is moving: RTS 22 Art 15 (notification, reconciliation, ARM-correction records) is UK-retained text flagged as revoked and replaced from 3 Apr 2028; the UK profile should reference the FCA Handbook successor once the 2026 consultation on rules finalises.
9. MiFIR feedback profile: WARN and ACPD statuses are marked "Not used" in TRI v1.6, and the instructions are NCA-dependent; keep status vocabularies in per-endpoint configuration (the brief already proposes this) rather than assuming the full status list.
