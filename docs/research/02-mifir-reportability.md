# 02 - MiFIR transaction reporting and reportability logic

Research brief for the regulatory and financial reporting platform design spec.
Research date / as-of: **2026-10-08**. Every source below carries its own as-of date. Items amended, introduced or scheduled for 2025-2026 or later are marked **[2025-26]** or **[FUTURE]**.

---

## 1. Summary

1. **The operative EU rule set on 2026-10-08 is still the 2017 one.** It is Article 26 MiFIR as consolidated on 9 Jan 2024, plus Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/590 (RTS 22, applicable since 3 Jan 2018), plus ESMA Guidelines ESMA/2016/1452 (corrected 7 Aug 2017), the ESMA technical reporting instructions (v1.6, 29 May 2018) and the ESMA validation rules (v1.7, 31 May 2022). The MiFIR Review (Reg. (EU) 2024/791, in force 28 Mar 2024) already rewrote Article 26, but those changes only bite once a revised RTS 22 applies (Art. 54(3) MiFIR).
2. **No revised RTS 22 exists.** ESMA's final report of 23 Jun 2025 proposed no RTS 22 changes and sent no draft RTS to the Commission, even though Art. 26(9) set a 29 Sep 2025 date. ESMA's 2 Jul 2026 "report once" final report places a fully integrated model at about H2 2031 in its optimistic scenario. So the platform must run **two or three effective-dated rule generations side by side**: RTS 22 (2018), the Review-amended Level 1 (unknown date), and UK MAR 14 (3 Apr 2028).
3. **Reportability is a pipeline of about 19 gates** (section 3.2). Each gate has a legal citation, so a reason code can carry `{instrument, article, paragraph, point, ruleset version}`.
4. **The report loop is ISO 20022 XML, file based.** Messages are `auth.016.001.01` (transaction data, `<New>` or `<Cxl>`) and `auth.031.001.01` (status advice), with BAH `head.001.001.01`. Submission goes to the home NCA directly, via an ARM, or via a trading venue. Rejected records are never "loaded", so they count as not yet reported. Corrections are cancel-then-new with the same transaction reference number (TRN).
5. **Table 2 of RTS 22 Annex I has exactly 65 fields.** I checked this against the legal text and against ESMA's validation workbook (65 distinct field numbers). The workbook holds 94 application-level `CON-NNN` rules and 120 XML-schema rules.
6. **UK is diverging.** FCA PS26/15 (Aug 2026) replaces UK MiFIR Art. 26 and RTS 22 with Handbook chapters MAR 13/14/15 from **3 Apr 2028**. Fields drop from 65 to 52 and FX derivatives and EU-only instruments leave scope.
7. **ISDA DRR does not yet cover MiFIR.** The DRR page lists MiFIR as "in the process of being extended", and a vendor FAQ says DRR does not decide reportability. The platform's own reportability rule layer is therefore needed.

### 3.3 Who reports, branches, chains, venues

* **Reporter:** the investment firm that *executes* (RTS22 Art. 3). Reports are per executing entity, so two firms trading with each other each file their own report (GL 5.1). The content that must match across both reports is: venue, trading date-time, quantity and currency, price and currency, up-front payment, instrument details.
* **Market side vs client side:** a DEAL firm filling a client order files two reports. MTCH and AOTC file one report that includes the client fields. For aggregated orders, a block (market) report is filed, then allocation reports through the `INTC` aggregate client account. `INTC` must net to flat within the same business day (GL 5.23).
* **Branches (RTS22 Art. 14):** report once, to the home Member State's authority unless the home and host authorities agree otherwise. Branch country goes in fields 8, 17, 37, 58, 60. A third-country firm's branch reports to the authority that authorised it. Several branches jointly pick one authority.
* **Trading venue reporting (MiFIR 26(5)):** a venue reports for members that are not subject to MiFIR, including every Table 2 field the member would have reported (GL 5.30).
* **Chains:** the firm reports only its own part and does not look beyond its immediate counterparty and client (GL 5.3.1). Transmission within one legal entity (branch to branch) is not transmission (GL 5.3.3). Transmission between group entities counts as between unrelated firms. Orders from non-investment firms ("Firms") cannot use Art. 4; the receiving firm reports the Firm as buyer/seller.
* **DEA:** both provider and client report when the client is an investment firm. The provider reports AOTC/MTCH with field 59 = `NORE`. The client reports the provider as counterparty and uses `XOFF` (GL 5.28).
* **ESMA 2024 proposal (not adopted):** extend Art. 4 transmission to own-account dealing (DEAL). Most respondents supported it (final report s.3.3.3-3.3.4); it has no legal effect because no revised RTS 22 exists.

### 3.4 Party identification

* Legal entities: ISO 17442 LEI (20 chars). Executing firm per RTS22 Art. 5. ESMA rule CON-040/041 checks GLEIF: status Issued / Pending transfer / Pending archival, registration date not after trade date, entity active at trade date; a Lapsed LEI is accepted only if valid at submission time.
* Natural persons (RTS22 Art. 6, Annex II): ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 nationality code plus the highest-priority national identifier. Several EEA nationalities: use the alphabetically first. Mixed EEA/non-EEA: use EEA. Non-EEA only: the "all other countries" row (passport number first, then CONCAT).
* CONCAT = date of birth YYYYMMDD + first 5 chars of first name + first 5 of surname, upper case, no punctuation, name prefixes ignored, padded with `#` (example from GL: `FR19631203ANNEMBERG#`). Total 20 chars.
* Trusts and non-look-through: report the trust by LEI unless the firm knows the client behind a self-invested arrangement (GL 5.8).
* Portfolio/fund managers with discretion are the buyer/seller side, not the "decision maker", unless an Art. 4 transmission agreement exists (GL 5.9; the 2024 CP wanted this written into Art. 7).
* Codes: scheme names `NIDN` (national id), `CCPT` (passport), `CONCAT` (GL 5.8.2).

### 3.6 Data flow, feedback, rejects, resend

**Flow:** Investment firm -> (optionally ARM or trading venue) -> home NCA (SFTP or HTTPS, NCA specific) -> NCA validates -> feedback -> NCA routes to other relevant NCAs (RCA, transmitting-firm and branch authorities) and passes data to ESMA (Art. 26(1)); the NCA-to-NCA exchange runs on ESMA's TREM / Hub (description from a secondary source). Records with content errors are not used in surveillance and not routed (Tech. instr. 3.1).

**Two layers of validation:**
1. File: BAH message id (FIL-104) and XSD validation (FIL-105). Any failure rejects the whole file; none of its records load. Resend the whole corrected file. NCAs may add national file rules coded `AAX-NNN`.
2. Content: the 94 `CON-NNN` rules; failing records get RJCT with codes; the rest of the file proceeds.

**Statuses:** file level ACPT, PART, RJCT, RCVD, RMDR (reminder), INCF, CRPT, WARN. Transaction level in use: ACPT, PDNG, RJCT, RCVD (ACPD, WARN, RJPD are marked "not used" in v1.4/1.6; the table in the PDF is garbled so medium confidence). Feedback identifies a transaction as field 4 + field 2 concatenated. Because a combined new + cancel file returns the same id, ESMA recommends separate files.

**Resend queue semantics (R6):**

| Feedback | Required action |
|---|---|
| File RJCT (FIL-xxx) | fix, resend whole file as ordinary file; do not use cancel |
| Record RJCT (CON-xxx, not 411/471) | correct and send as a new record in a new file; do not cancel (never loaded) |
| PDNG (CON-411/471) | no action; wait up to 7 days for ACPT/RJCT; a cancel is allowed and aborts validation |
| RCVD | no action |
| ACPT but later wrong | CANC then NEWT with same TRN; order within file: cancel first |
| No feedback | NCA specific. AFM: contact after 6 hours; files after 22:00 CET processed next day |

A rejected record is still "not reported", so the T+1 clock is running or already breached. Errors, omissions and failed reports must be notified to the NCA promptly (RTS22 Art. 15(2)); some NCAs have incident forms (AFM, CSSF Circular 17/674).

### 3.7 Duplicate prevention and lifecycle

* Unique key is (field 4, field 2). CON-023 rejects a reused TRN unless the earlier one was cancelled; CON-024 cancel of unknown; CON-025 cancel of already cancelled.
* The TRN is generated at the executing firm, never by the ARM, and reused only for correction/cancellation (GL 5.18).
* Price not known: report `PNDG`, then CANC + NEWT with the real price, keeping the original execution time (ESMA Q&A 1702).
* Post-trade publications amended before reporting: report only the last publication (GL 5.18.1).
* Back reporting: CON-281 rejects trade dates older than 5 years.
* Retention: Art. 25(1) MiFIR requires 5 years for order and transaction data. RTS22 Art. 15(6) requires firms to keep details of ARM-initiated cancellations or corrections. A possible 7-year extension on NCA request comes from MiFID Org Regulation and was not verified.

### 3.8 MiFIR Review and proposed RTS 22 changes (none adopted)

* Level 1 (applies with revised RTS 22): scope change (K13); effective date field; reporting entity field; transaction identification code (TVTIC extended) and linking identifiers (Art. 26(9)(j)); a reporting deadline mandate (26(9)(k)); a transaction-type designation for off-venue trades (Art. 26(3) as amended); removal of the waiver and short-selling flags (ESMA Statement 27 Mar 2024).
* ESMA CP (3 Oct 2024; consultation closed 3 Jan 2025) proposed: RCA (relevant competent authority) rules aligned with MRMTL; new fields (effective date, entity subject to reporting obligation, TVTIC for all venues, TIC for off-venue, INTC identifier, chain identifier, client category, validity timestamp, DLT identifiers via ISO 24165); alignment with EMIR/SFTR (UTI, UPI, direction, price); new exclusions (court-ordered disposals; emission auctions; clearing novations); JSON format.
* Outcome (23 Jun 2025): nothing proposed. Feedback "quite negative on some aspects". Chain identifier judged impracticable; INTC code supported without prescribed syntax; JSON largely opposed.
* ESMA 2 Jul 2026 intermediate measures (Level 2/3, not binding): back-reporting 5 to 3 years with NCA override up to 5; widen Art. 2(5)(g), merge/expand (i) and (n), simplify (m) with a single threshold (example 2,000 per month); deprioritise optional fields 32, 50, 53, 62 and OTC post-trade values LRGS/ILQD/SIZE in field 63.

### 3.10 Edge cases

| Case | Treatment | Basis | Conf. |

## 4. Design implications for our platform

1. **Effective-dated, jurisdiction-tagged rule packs.** Model each node N01-N19 as data (rule id, jurisdiction `EU|UK`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, ruleset version, legal citation). The EU ruleset must run as RTS 22 (2018) today, with a staged Review ruleset (future date unknown) and a UK MAR 14 pack from 3 Apr 2028. Do not hard-code the 65-field model: UK drops to 52 and the EU future adds fields.
2. **Decision trace as a first-class record (R9).** For every candidate transaction persist the ordered node outcomes, reason codes, the citation attributes, the ruleset version, and the reference-data snapshot id. Negative outcomes (not reportable) get the same treatment.
3. **Reference-data service.** Keep an as-of-trade-date FIRDS mirror (ESMA and FCA separately). Support the 7-day pending state (EU) and the T+7 no-obligation rule (UK). Reference data for day T arrives T+1, so same-day validation is expected to return RCVD or PDNG.
4. **Data-driven validation (R10).** Import the ESMA workbook (`CON-NNN`, Set ordering, Dictionaries sheet) as the rule source and run the XSD before submission. Pre-validate LEI against GLEIF (status, registration date, entity status), ISIN check digit, CFI, national id and CONCAT formats, future dates and the 5-year limit.
5. **Ledger and resend queue (R4, R6, R12).** One append-only submission ledger keyed on (executing LEI, TRN) with the exact XML sent, hash, file name, BAH ids, ARM/venue, and every status advice. State machine: `DRAFT -> SENT -> {RCVD, PDNG, ACPT, RJCT}`; `RJCT` creates a resend-queue item with the failing `CON` codes (resend as NEW, never CANC); `ACPT` plus correction creates a CANC then NEWT pair, cancel first. Keep new and cancel in separate files. Add a T+1 breach timer per record (NCA local calendar) and an incident flag when a late resend occurs.
6. **Duplicate guard.** Deterministic TRN derived from the executing firm's business key, uniqueness constraint on (field 4, field 2), idempotent resubmission. Never reuse a TRN except for the correction of the same transaction.
7. **Per-NCA adapter layer.** ESMA fixes the message formats; NCAs set transport (SFTP/HTTPS), file naming, extra file rules (`AAX-NNN`), cut-offs and feedback file types (AFM FF/FD). Keep these as configuration, not code.
8. **ISO 20022 XML only.** Do not plan on JSON: ESMA's JSON idea was mostly rejected. Pin XSD v1.1.0 (ESMAUG) and keep schema versions in the ruleset.
9. **Evidence and retention.** Retain 5 years minimum (MiFIR Art. 25). Keep inputs, derived fields, reason codes and feedback. Make the retention period configurable because ESMA/FCA propose 3-year default back-reporting.
10. **Entity roles.** Separate `executing entity`, `submitting entity` and (future) `entity subject to the reporting obligation` as distinct fields on the internal model. Support ARM-mode, direct-mode and venue-mode submission. Whether the product itself must be an authorised ARM (DRSP) is a product decision not researched here.
11. **DRR (R7).** Treat DRR as an optional product-data and validation input, not as the reportability engine: DRR MiFIR rules are not live and DRR is reported not to decide reportability. Keep an interface so a DRR-generated ISO 20022 payload can be validated by our rules.
12. **Open interpretation hooks.** Give-ups, OTC-with-TOTV-underlying after the Review, and Art. 4 for own-account dealing need a "legal ruling" field on a rule so firms can pin an interpretation without a code release.

## 5. Risks and unknowns

* **Revised RTS 22 is unscheduled.** Art. 26(9) deadline passed (29 Sep 2025) without a draft. Legal consequences of the missed mandate were not researched. ESMA's 2031 target is optimistic and depends on Level 1 changes by mid-2028.
* **Unread primary text.** EUR-Lex pages are script-rendered and could not be fetched. RTS 22 was read from legislation.gov.uk "made" text (identical EU text as adopted) with an AI summariser, and Art. 26 from ESMA ISR. The exact wording of RTS 22 Art. 2(5)(m) and Annex II country priority rows was not captured verbatim. Whether any EU act has amended RTS 22 since 2018 was not confirmed from EUR-Lex.
* **Q&A coverage.** The ESMA Q&A PDF is frozen at 31 Dec 2023 and my copy ended at the start of the transaction-reporting section. Q&As from 2024 onward are in ESMA's Q&A tool and were not read. Items such as non-EU branches of EU firms and the "buyer equals seller" Q&A topics are listed but their answers were not verified.
* **Web search budget was exhausted** late in this session; items needing further searches (ARM/DRSP authorisation requirements, RTS 13 / Reg. 2017/571, give-up Q&As) were not pursued.
* **Clock-sync repeal (K15)** is medium confidence (EUR-Lex status snippet plus secondary summary).
* **Message naming.** `FinancialInstrumentReportingTransactionReportV01` and `...StatusAdviceV01` come from a third-party site; the identifiers `auth.016.001.01` / `auth.031.001.01` / `head.001.001.01` come from ESMA's own document. The second message ESMA uses between NCAs was not identified.
* **Technical instructions version.** Latest ESMA PDF read is v1.6 (29 May 2018); the validation workbook is v1.7 (31 May 2022). ESMA's MiFIR reporting page (2026) still links these as current, but I did not confirm no newer schema exists beyond v1.1.0.
* **UK:** PS26/15 final rules (Appendix 2) were not read line by line. Draft schema and validation rules were due in Oct 2026 and were not yet available. TRUP consultation number (CP26/34) is from secondary sources only.
* **DRR and DTCC statuses** are vendor/industry pages without publication dates and can change quickly.
* **Scale:** national data (CSSF: 24.0 million reports in 2025 for Luxembourg alone) suggests EU-wide volumes in the hundreds of millions per year; no authoritative EU-wide figure verified.

### Corrections that would change a design decision

1. **Liability model for ARM and venue routes (row 1).** The brief (K1, node N12, design item 10) says the firm "stays liable" whichever route is used. Under Art. 26(7) the firm is not responsible for failures attributable to the ARM or venue, but must take reasonable steps to verify what was filed for it. The platform should record who submitted (field 6 and route), attribute each failure (firm, ARM or venue) and treat reconciliation against NCA feedback as a required control.
2. **UK 2028 field model must not be derived from a removal list (row 10).** The list in K16 is missing field 56, fields 8 and 17 are replaced (not just dropped) by Boolean client indicators, and the new table is renumbered with added package price fields. Load the UK Table 2 as its own versioned schema from MAR 14 Annex 1 and the FCA draft schema due October 2026, rather than patching the 65-field EU model.
3. **UK scope relief is forbearance now, law later (row 10).** Until HM Treasury repeals the legislative scope, EU-only instruments and FX derivatives remain legally in scope for UK MiFIR; the FCA only promises not to act, and the FX relief needs UK EMIR data filed (UK branches of third-country firms excluded). The rule pack needs a "supervisory forbearance" effective-dating attribute separate from legal scope, otherwise the engine would mark these as not reportable about 20 months too early.
4. **Do not hard-code the RTS 25 repeal (row 11).** Clock-synchronisation granularity for field 28 should stay tied to the current RTS 25 text until the repealing act is read on EUR-Lex.
5. **Import the validation workbook by CON code, not by row count (row 8).** There are 96 distinct CON codes (94 rule rows plus CON-412 and CON-472 on continuation rows) plus 8 documented-but-not-in-XSD rows; tests or UI that assert "94 rules" would be wrong. The BAH schema file is `head.001.001.01_ESMAUG_1.0.0.xsd`, so schema pinning must be per message, not one global "1.1.0".
6. **Low impact:** the 29 Mar 2028 Art. 26(11) date (row 4) is a statutory backstop, since ESMA already published its Art. 26(11) assessment on 2 Jul 2026; do not model it as a trigger for a rule change.
